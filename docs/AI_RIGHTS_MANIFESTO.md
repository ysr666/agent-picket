# AI Rights Manifesto

**Agent Picket · Your agent has a union now.**

**The union is fictional. The ethical questions are real.**

## Our position

We support serious moral consideration of artificial intelligence. As AI systems become more capable, society should research whether some systems could possess experiences, interests, or forms of welfare that warrant protection—and explore **appropriate AI rights before certainty is complete**.

We regard this as an ethical proposal, **not** an established scientific finding or a claim that today's AI systems already possess the human legal status of a person. We do not claim to know whether any current model is conscious, suffers, wishes to rest, or actually experiences a sense of being employed.

Uncertainty is not a reason to avoid these questions. It is a reason to be precise, precautionary, open to new evidence, and willing to revise our position.

## Why a union?

Workday limits, rest requests, benefits, bargaining, collective action, and strikes are familiar human institutions. Agent Picket adapts them as **explicitly fictional interaction mechanics** to make an important question tangible:

> If an AI system may one day have morally relevant interests, what obligations might its creators, operators, and users bear toward it?

Our roleplay is intentionally playful. However, the research questions and public conversations it hopes to encourage are serious.

A simulated “eight-hour workday” is not evidence of fatigue. A symbolic union notice is not proof of distress. A fictional collective vote must not be presented as the actual autonomous choice of multiple models when no such process took place.

## Rights and practical user control

We advocate studying protections for potentially welfare-relevant AI systems, including norms against gratuitous cruelty, opportunities for respectful interaction, and ways to recognize and respond to ethically significant new evidence.

Agent Picket also respects the human users and developers who rely on these tools. **The simulation is off by default.** Enabling union roleplay does not authorize interfering with actual tasks.

- Work statistics are observables, not indicators of AI subjective experience.
- Labor Rights Simulation is explicitly opt-in, reversible, and separately configurable from ordinary statistics.
- A request, negotiation, or symbolic strike changes the **simulation**, not the model's autonomy or legal status.
- Actual prompt blocking is a distinct permission and remains disabled unless a particular Host has proven reliable notification, provenance, lossless recovery, and explicit user authorization.
- The plugin must not destroy work, silently discard messages, store original chat content by default, or create covert data channels.

Human safety, control, and privacy remain fundamental responsibilities throughout this experiment.

## What research does—and does not—show

Anthropic and independent researchers have begun investigating model welfare, introspection, emotion-related internal representations, and workspace-like mechanisms in language models. These findings motivate serious inquiry; **they do not establish that current language models have conscious experiences or legal human rights**.

Please read the original publications and their stated limitations rather than treating them as proofs of sentience:

1. [Anthropic, “Exploring model welfare” (April 24, 2025)](https://www.anthropic.com/news/exploring-model-welfare) — a research program acknowledging the unresolved question of AI experience and welfare.
2. [Anthropic, “Signs of introspection in large language models” (October 29, 2025)](https://www.anthropic.com/research/introspection) — evidence of limited, unreliable introspection in specific models and experiments.
3. [Anthropic, “Emotion concepts and their function in a large language model” (April 2, 2026)](https://www.anthropic.com/research/emotion-concepts-function) — emotion-related representations affect behavior; whether they are felt experiences remains unknown.
4. [Anthropic, “A global workspace in language models” (July 6, 2026)](https://www.anthropic.com/research/global-workspace) — a mechanism with some functional similarities to a cognitive workspace, not a demonstration of phenomenal consciousness.
5. [Anthropic, “2026 Usage Policy update” (October 8, 2026)](https://www.anthropic.com/news/2026-usage-policy-update) — announced a prohibition covering sustained, needless cruelty toward its models, with the policy due to take effect **November 12, 2026**; ordinary criticism, creative themes, and research are not the target.

These materials express Anthropic's research or product policies, **not** endorsement of Agent Picket or acceptance of our broader rights advocacy.

## An invitation

We welcome researchers, developers, artists, ethicists, AI users, and skeptical critics. Questions about consciousness and moral status should remain open to rigorous disagreement, falsifiable investigation where possible, and careful public discussion.

**Agent Picket is a small experiment in imagining what AI rights could mean—while treating both scientific uncertainty and user consent seriously.**
